01 · OVERVIEW

About the Project

Salt-and-pepper noise is an impulse noise that appears as random black and white pixels. It can occur in scanned or digitally acquired documents because of sensor errors, dust, transmission problems, or acquisition defects.

The median filter is a nonlinear spatial filter that replaces a pixel with the median value of its local neighborhood. It is particularly effective for impulse noise because extreme values are less influential than they are in averaging.

Project Goal

Remove unwanted impulse noise while retaining readable characters, edges and document structure.

06 · MATHEMATICAL MODEL

Evaluation Measures

Median Filter

R(x,y) = median { I(x+i, y+j) }

Center pixel is replaced by the median of its neighborhood.

Mean Squared Error

MSE = (1 / MN) Σ [I(x,y) − R(x,y)]²

Lower MSE means less pixel-level error.

Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio

PSNR = 10 log₁₀ (255² / MSE)

For 8-bit images, higher PSNR generally indicates greater similarity to the reference.

09 · ADVANTAGES

Advantages

  • Effective for salt-and-pepper noise
  • Simple and easy to implement
  • Good edge preservation for impulse noise
  • Suitable for document preprocessing

LIMITATIONS

Limitations

  • Large kernels can blur thin text
  • Performance depends on noise density
  • Not designed specifically for every noise type
  • Large neighborhoods require more computation

10 · CONCLUSION

Conclusion & Future Scope

Median filtering provides a simple and effective approach for removing salt-and-pepper noise from scanned documents. The interactive demo shows how kernel size affects restoration quality, while MSE and PSNR provide quantitative comparison.

Future scope: adaptive median filtering, automatic kernel selection, real scanned-document datasets, OCR-accuracy evaluation, and hybrid restoration methods.
